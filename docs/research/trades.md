# The city at work (AD 113)

Background for `src/npc/crowd/trades.ts`: the shops, workshops and daily routines the crowd stations stage. It is a companion to `society.md`, which covers Martial's day, the markets and the cart ban.

## The day's rhythm
- **Before dawn.**
  - Bakers are already at work. Martial 12.57 complains that in Rome a poor man can't think or sleep: schoolmasters in the morning, bakers at night, coppersmiths' hammers all day.
  - Clients in togas queue at their patrons' doors for the *salutatio* (Martial 3.36, 4.8; Juvenal 1.95–126). A doorkeeper (*ostiarius*) lets them in, and the reward is the *sportula* dole.
- **Morning (hours 1–6).**
  - Business, the courts and the markets.
  - Schoolmasters (*ludi magister*) teach boys in the street under an awning or a portico from before cockcrow (Martial 9.68).
  - Sacrifices are made at the altars before the temples, always with a flute-player (*tibicen*) to cover ill-omened words.
  - The monthly grain dole (*frumentatio*) is handed out at the Porticus Minucia to the ~200,000 registered citizens with their tokens.
- **Midday (hours 6–7).** *Prandium* and rest. Shops stay open, but the streets thin out.
- **Afternoon (hour 8 on).** The baths open, and the whole city goes. Martial 14.163 has the bell (*aes thermarum*) that rings the opening, and 10.48 says "the eighth hour". The Baths of Trajan opened in 109.
- **Evening and night.** Dinner (*cena*). The thermopolia and popinae stay open, and dice are played in the back rooms (gambling was illegal outside the Saturnalia and tolerated anyway). Wheeled carts are banned by day (Lex Iulia Municipalis), so the streets fill with them at night. The vigiles patrol with buckets and axes.

## Trades and where they were
- **Booksellers (librarii):**
  - Martial's bookseller Atrectus kept a shop in the Argiletum, its door-posts covered with the titles on sale (1.117).
  - Secundus sold Martial's book behind the Temple of Peace (1.2).
  - The Sosii brothers (Horace) were by the Vertumnus statue in the Vicus Tuscus.
- **Cobblers:** the Argiletum and the *Vicus Sandalarius* (Sandal-makers' Street).
- **Barbers (tonsores):**
  - They worked in the street until Domitian's edict cleared the stalls from the pavements (Martial 7.61).
  - A trip to the barber was a social hour, and they were famous gossips.
- **Fullers (fullonicae):**
  - Cloth was trodden in vats of water, fuller's earth and urine (*saltus fullonicus*), then rinsed, brushed and bleached with sulphur.
  - Vespasian taxed the urine collected from public latrines ("pecunia non olet").
- **Bakers (pistores):**
  - Bakeries milled their own flour on hourglass-shaped lava mills turned by donkeys or slaves (Pompeii, Apuleius *Met.* 9.11–13).
  - The bread went into a domed oven and was sold at the door.
  - The tomb of the baker Eurysaces at the Porta Maggiore shows the whole process.
- **Thermopolia and popinae:** masonry counters with *dolia* sunk in them for hot food and wine (Pompeii, Herculaneum, Ostia). Aediles and emperors kept trying to limit what they could serve hot.
- **Velabrum and Vicus Tuscus:**
  - Oil, food and perfume.
  - The Velabrum's smoked cheese (Martial 11.52, 13.32).
  - Silks and fine cloth (*vestiarii Tusci*).
- **Via Sacra:**
  - Jewellers and pearl-dealers (*margaritarii*): the Porticus Margaritaria.
  - Spices from the Horrea Piperataria, Domitian's pepper warehouse.
- **Saepta Julia:** luxury dealers in slaves, Corinthian bronzes, citrus-wood tables and tortoiseshell. Martial 9.59: Mamurra handles everything in the Saepta and buys two cups for an as.
- **Markets:**
  - Fish and meat at the Macellum Liviae on the Esquiline and the Macellum Magnum on the Caelian.
  - Vegetables at the Forum Holitorium.
  - Cattle at the Forum Boarium.
- **The river:** the Portus Tiberinus and, below the Aventine, the Emporium and the Porticus Aemilia. Porters (*saccarii*) carried amphorae and sacks up from the barges.
- **The Circus factions:** Greens, Blues, Whites and Reds. Fans argued about them in the street (Juvenal 11.197–202; Pliny, *Ep.* 9.6, is bored by it). Scorpus, the charioteer of Domitian's day, is still the name to drop.
- **The Ludus Magnus:**
  - Gladiators trained at the *palus* (a post) with wooden swords heavier than real ones (Vegetius 1.11).
  - They ate barley and beans: *hordearii*, barley-men (Pliny *NH* 18.72).
  - Their doctors (*doctores*) were specialists by armatura.
  - The school had its own smiths, a mess and an infirmary.
- **The fleet:** sailors of the Misenum fleet, quartered in the Castra Misenatium by the Colosseum, worked its awning (*velarium*).
