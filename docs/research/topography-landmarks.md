# SKYROME — Landmark Gazetteer: Rome, AD 113

Research reference for world-building agents. **184 landmarks** that stand or are being built in Rome in spring–summer AD 113 (866 AUC): **94** are priority 1, **60** priority 2 and **30** priority 3. Each has a position in the SKYROME world frame, an orientation, rough dimensions, its status in 113, lore and builder notes.

> Scope: the city of Rome only. Ostia/Portus, Latium and the provinces come later. Fantasy is toned down: lore mentions *beliefs* (omens, ghosts, curses) and never confirms them.
> Every number marked **FLAG** or rated `conf C` is an informed estimate, not a measured fact. Treat it as a placeholder that may move.

---

## 1. Conventions (read first)

### 1.1 World frame
- **Units:** real metres, local tangent plane.
- **Origin `[0, 0]`:** the **Miliarium Aureum** (Golden Milestone). This is the OSM-mapped marble remains at the S end of (just behind) the Rostra, beside the Temple of Saturn: **lat0 = 41.892580, lon0 = 12.484380** (OSM way 131557543, "Miliarum Aureum"). Other agents should use **exactly these constants**.
- **Axes:** `+x = EAST`, `+z = SOUTH` (north is `-z`), and `y` is up (three.js convention).
- **Conversion (as specified for the project):**
  - `x = (lon - lon0) * 111320 * cos(lat0)`, which is about `(lon - 12.484380) * 82866.4`
  - `z = -(lat - lat0) * 110574`
  - Inverse: `lon = lon0 + x / 82866.4`, `lat = lat0 - z / 110574`
- **Game scale:** the game multiplies layout and landmark dimensions by `WORLD_SCALE = 0.6`. Human-scale items (doors, steps, furniture, generic insulae) stay 1:1. Every number in this file is **real-world, unscaled**.

### 1.3 How positions were derived
1. **OpenStreetMap** footprints of surviving remains (Overpass API, 2026-10-03). Centres and orientations come from a minimum-area oriented bounding box; the Colosseum uses an ellipse fit (axes 190 x 161 m at 109°, against a published 188 x 156).
2. **OpenHistoricalMap** reconstructed ancient footprints (features extant in 113), used as a cross-check and for buildings without remains.
3. **Pleiades** gazetteer representative points, and **Wikipedia** coordinates.
4. Positions for unlocated buildings come from literary and topographic evidence (Regionary catalogues, Marble Plan, Platner & Ashby, Richardson, Coarelli) and are always `conf C`.
5. Orientation, size and height come from published measurements where available (cited in notes). Otherwise they are estimated and **FLAG**ged.

### 1.5 Key 113 timeline facts used below
- **1 Jan 112:** Forum of Trajan and Basilica Ulpia dedicated.
- **12 May 113:** Trajan's Column dedicated and the Temple of Venus Genetrix (Forum of Caesar) rededicated on the same day (Fasti Ostienses).
- **June 109:** Baths of Trajan dedicated. **109:** Aqua Traiana. **Nov 109:** Naumachia of Trajan.
- **110:** Pantheon burned by lightning. Rebuilding is generally dated from **c. 114** (brick stamps; Hetland), so in 113 it is a burned shell or construction site.
- **Autumn 113:** Trajan leaves for the Parthian war, so the Janus doors are open and the Temple of Mars Ultor and Temple of Bellona are politically hot.
- **104:** fire on the Oppian. The Domus Aurea wing is buried as the platform for the Baths of Trajan.
- **96:** Domitian murdered; *damnatio memoriae* (his statues, arches and the Equus Domitiani removed). Nerva (96–98) dedicated the Forum Transitorium in 97 and is buried in the Mausoleum of Augustus.

The civic heart. Imperial paving lies at roughly 12-13 m asl. The square's long axis runs about 115/295 deg, from the Temple of Divus Julius (ESE) to the Capitoline (WNW). The Sacra Via runs along it (see Appendix C).

### B. Capitoline Hill

Two summits: the Capitolium (S, ~45 m asl) and the Arx (N, ~49 m), with the Asylum saddle between them (~38-40 m). The Clivus Capitolinus climbs from the Forum past Saturn to the Area Capitolina. Its main gate is in the middle of the SE side.

### E. Velia, Colosseum Valley and Oppian

The Sacra Via crests at the Velia (~27 m asl) by the Arch of Titus. It then descends to the Colosseum plaza (~20 m) where several regions meet at the Meta Sudans. The Oppian (Baths of Titus and Trajan, ~38-45 m) rises to the NE and the Caelian (~40 m) to the SE.

## Appendix D. Uncertainty register (largest open questions)

1. **Facing directions that are debated:** Temple of Saturn (NE assumed), Capitolium (SSE assumed, ±15°), Apollo Palatinus (S/SW "generally believed"), Magna Mater (SW assumed), Templum Pacis (aedes assumed to face NW), Veiovis, Juno Moneta, Sant'Omobono twins, Bellona (roughly S), Iseum (N-S axis; front unknown), Castra Praetoria main gate. Builders should treat these as art-direction choices that can be flipped later.
2. **Unlocated buildings** (`conf C`), placed by best guess: Temple of Divus Augustus, Janus Geminus, Lupercal, Pulvinar, Temple of Sol in the circus, Temple of Ceres, Ludi Dacicus/Matutinus/Gallicus, Castra Misenatium, Moneta, Macellum Magnum, Porticus Minucia Frumentaria, Diribitorium, Divorum, Ustrinum, Porticus Vipsania, Aventine temples (Diana, Minerva, Juno Regina), Privata Traiani, Thermae Suranae, Temple of Quirinus, Templum Gentis Flaviae, Naumachiae of Augustus and Trajan, Pons Sublicius, Porta Carmentalis/Trigemina, Colossus spot on the Velia.
3. **State in 113:** Pantheon (shell vs. early works), Basilica of Neptune (fire damage of 110?), Naumachia of Augustus (filled after 80?), Circus Vaticanus (in use?), Diribitorium (roofless since 80), Velia vestibule (what Neronian porticoes survived), Atrium Vestae and Ludus Magnus (Trajanic works complete or ongoing), Umbilicus (Severan remains), House of Augustus (preserved or absorbed).
4. **Heights** of most temples are estimates from column heights and typical proportions (podium + columns ≈ 10 × lower column diameter + entablature + pediment). Capitolium height especially uncertain (~35 m).
5. **Ground levels (`gnd`)** are rough. Ancient levels around the Circus, Forum Boarium and Campus are 4–9 m below modern ones. Always defer to the terrain DEM.
6. **Insula dell'Ara Coeli** may postdate 113; it is kept as the insula *template*.
7. **Temple of Divus Traianus site:** recent excavation under Palazzo Valentini questions whether a temple was ever there. For 113 leave an open court either way.
