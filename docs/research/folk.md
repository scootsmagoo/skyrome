# What the people of Rome talk about (May 113)

Background for `src/content/folk/`. Each unnamed person in the crowd is given a persona (name, status, trade, home, birthplace, family, mood), and from it a handful of topics answered in their own voice.

## Who is in the street
- **Status sets the name and the voice.**
  - Freeborn citizens have three names (*tria nomina*). Women take the family name plus a cognomen.
  - Freed slaves take their former master's praenomen and nomen and keep their slave name as a cognomen. A *Marcus Ulpius* is a freedman of Trajan's own family.
  - Slaves have one name: Greek (Eros, Philemon) or hopeful Latin (Felix, Fortunatus, Hilarus).
  - Foreigners keep their own names and say where they come from (Antioch, Smyrna, Alexandria).
- **Slavery in the city:**
  - Most urban slaves expected freedom eventually: often around thirty, in the master's will, or bought with the *peculium* (savings a master let them keep).
  - Freedom was taxed at a twentieth of the slave's value (*vicesima libertatis*).
  - Freedmen owed their former master (*patronus*) respect and services. Many got rich in trade, the stereotype Petronius mocks with Trimalchio.
- **The plebs:**
  - **The dole:** citizens on the list collected five modii of grain a month at the Porticus Minucia.
  - **Clients:** the poorer of them stood in their patron's queue at dawn for the *salutatio* and the *sportula*, 100 quadrantes or 6¼ sestertii (Martial 1.59, 3.7).
  - **Housing:** most lived in rented rooms in tall *insulae*: dark, expensive, prone to collapse and fire. The poorest lived highest, under the tiles (Juvenal 3.190–222).
- **The destitute:**
  - Beggars sat at bridges and on temple steps (Juvenal 4.116, 5.8; Martial 10.5).
  - Sick slaves were abandoned on Tiber Island. Claudius ruled that those who recovered were free (Suetonius, *Claudius* 25).

## Money (to keep the lines consistent)
- **Coins:** 1 denarius = 4 sestertii = 16 asses.
- **Food and drink:**
  - A pound loaf cost about 2 asses.
  - A Pompeian tavern sign prices wine at 1 as, 2 for the better, 4 for Falernian (CIL IV 1679).
- **Wages and prices:**
  - A day labourer earned 3–4 sestertii.
  - A legionary was paid 1,200 sestertii a year after Domitian.
  - An ordinary slave sold for one to two and a half thousand sestertii.
- **Rent:** Juvenal says a year's rent for a dark room in Rome would buy a house in Sora or Fabrateria (3.223–5).

## What people talked about in May 113
- **The Column:** dedicated 12 May 113 in Trajan's Forum, its spiral frieze telling the Dacian wars. People expected (correctly) that his ashes would rest in its base.
- **Parthia:**
  - The Parthian king Osroes had put his own nominee on the throne of Armenia.
  - Trajan left for the East in the autumn of 113.
  - Recruiting, mule prices and envoys were the news.
- **The succession:**
  - Trajan, nearly sixty, had no son and named no heir.
  - Hadrian, married to his grandniece Sabina and favoured by Plotina, was the likely choice; Servianus was a rival.
  - It was a dangerous thing to ask aloud.
- **The emperor:**
  - He was popular: the *Optimus* legend, walking in the city on foot, the baths (109), markets, forum and Aqua Traiana (109).
  - The 123 days of games after Dacia (Dio 68.15).
  - He liked wine (Dio 68.7).
  - The *alimenta*: loans to Italian landowners whose interest fed poor children in the towns. The Veleia tablet gives 16 sestertii a month for a boy and 12 for a girl.
- **Games and the Circus:**
  - Gladiators were idols ("Celadus, the girls' heart-throb", a Pompeii graffito).
  - The factions (Greens, Blues, Whites, Reds) divided families (Pliny *Ep.* 9.6; Juvenal 11.197).
- **Daily misery:**
  - Fire, collapsing buildings, the Tiber's floods.
  - The night-time carts that kept the city awake: "insomnia kills more men than fever here" (Juvenal 3.232–8).
  - Muggers after dark, and pots falling from windows.
- **Gods and the dead:**
  - The Lemuria (9, 11 and 13 May): black beans thrown at midnight to send the dead away (Ovid, *Fasti* 5.419–92).
  - Crossroads shrines; private cults (Isis, the Great Mother, Mithras among soldiers).
  - The new sect Pliny reported from Bithynia c. 112: the Christians, who "sang a hymn to Christ as to a god" before dawn. Trajan replied that they were not to be hunted down, nor anonymous accusations accepted (*Ep.* 10.96–97).
- **Foreigners:** Rome was a city of immigrants; Juvenal grumbles that "the Syrian Orontes has long since flowed into the Tiber" (3.62). Greek doctors, tutors and astrologers were indispensable and resented.
- **Doctors:** they were mostly Greek and distrusted. Martial's Diaulus "was a doctor, now he's an undertaker: what he does as an undertaker, he did as a doctor" (1.47). The poor went to Aesculapius on Tiber Island instead.
- **Love:** the walls of Pompeii are full of it: "Successus the weaver loves the innkeeper's slave girl Iris, who cares nothing for him" (CIL IV 8259).
- **Elders remember:**
  - Nero's fire (64);
  - the year of the four emperors (69), when the Capitol burned;
  - the inauguration of the Colosseum under Titus (100 days of games, 80);
  - the fear under Domitian.
