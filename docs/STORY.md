# Skyrome: the story (Act I as played)

This is the story as the player meets it. `docs/GDD.md` §10 is the design of the whole main quest, and `docs/CONTENT.md` §3 has the quest specs. Where this file and those two disagree, this file wins: it was written after a playtest found the opening murky ("why was I attacked? why am I fighting gladiators?").

## The rules this story keeps
1. **Every scene answers three questions.** Who wants something, why, and what the player should do next. Each character says it in plain words.
2. **Nothing happens at random in the opening.** The first fight is set up beforehand: Festus says he is being followed. The killer is seen, and he runs with something. Street muggers stay off the streets until Act I's opening night is over.
3. **One thread at a time.** The main quest never needs a side quest to go on. Side quests show up as offers and never take over the tracker.
4. **The next step is always on screen.**
   - The objective tracker and the gold marker point at the next step, and the marker rides on the person if it's a person.
   - A banner announces each new objective.
   - The journal tells the story in the first person, past tense.
5. **History holds** (AD 113, 11 May, the eve of the Column's dedication). Fictional people do fictional things in real places. Real people are seen but don't speak.

## Background (what is true before the game starts)
- **The war:** Trajan is about to go to war with Parthia. Couriers ride between Rome and the army gathering in Syria.
- **The couriers:** the *frumentarii*, soldiers seconded as imperial couriers and, quietly, as the emperor's informers. Their camp is the Castra Peregrina on the Caelian.
- **The plot:** someone in Rome is taking Parthian silver to make trouble at the dedication of Trajan's Column on 12 May.
- **Festus and his twin:**
  - A courier, **Gaius Marius Festus**, picked up a warning in Antioch. He is carrying it home in his own cipher, because he has learned that his camp leaks.
  - Only his twin brother, **Gemellus**, a copyist, knows the cipher (a game the boys played to hide their notes from their father).
  - Gemellus vanished in April after a client frightened him. He is hiding in the back room of Tryphon's bookshop.

## Chapter 1: The Dripping Gate (`mq-01-madida-capena`)
*Porta Capena, 11 May, 04:30.*

1. **The cart.** The player rides in on the last night wine-cart. The courier Festus shares the cart and opens the conversation by himself. He says:
   - he carries a sealed dispatch for his centurion;
   - two riders have kept pace with the cart since Bovillae, never passing and never falling back;
   - he'd like company through the gate: "Two are harder to knife than one. There's a denarius in it."

   **Objective:** Walk through the Capena Gate with Festus. He walks beside the player.
2. **The ambush.** Under the dripping arch three men step out. A hooded man with a curved blade knifes Festus and runs off up the Circus valley with his satchel. The other two turn on the player ("Get the tablet! It's not in the bag!"). The tutorial hints for fighting play here.
   **Objective:** Fight off the attackers.
3. **Festus dying.** He presses the sealed tablet into the player's hand.
   - "They knew my road and my hour. Only the camp knew that. Don't take it to the camp."
   - "Take it to Gratus, my centurion. He keeps an office in the strongrooms under the Temple of Castor, in the Forum. Only Gratus."
   - "The one with the curved blade took my satchel. Up from under, like a gladiator."
   - "Tell my mother. The Marii, lamp-makers, in the Velabrum."

   **Objectives:** Hear Festus's last words. Optional: ask the carter Dromo what he saw (the hooded man walked like a fighter and ran toward the Circus).
4. **Chapter complete.** Chapter 2 begins at once.

## Chapter 2: The Sealed Tablet (`mq-02-tabella`)
*The Forum, the Ludus Magnus and the Vicus Tuscus. 11 May, from dawn to dusk.*

1. **The road.** Take the tablet to Gratus. The marker leads up the Circus valley, through the Velabrum and the Vicus Tuscus, to the Temple of Castor in the Forum. *Optional:* search Festus's body (his purse and an unsent letter home).
2. **The keeper.** The temple is shut for the Lemuria, but the strongrooms in its podium are not. Their keeper, **Chrysippus**, doesn't give out his clients' names. Showing him Festus's seal (a horseman with a spear: the couriers' mark) sends him running for Gratus. A bribe or a good word works too.
3. **Gratus** (centurion of the frumentarii) hears how Festus died and lays it out:
   - "Festus's road and hour were known only in our camp. Someone there sold him."
   - "If I'm seen taking that tablet in the Forum in daylight, whoever paid for Festus knows it reached me. Keep it on you, and bring it after sunset, when the Forum's empty."
   - "Meanwhile, the man who did it. A curved blade, up from under: that's how a thraex finishes a man on his knees. Gladiators. Go to the Ludus Magnus by the Amphitheatre and ask for Glaucus, their chief trainer. He knows every man who ever held a sica in Rome. Get me a name."
   - Why the player? "Because nobody knows your face. And because Festus chose you."
4. **Glaucus** at the Ludus names the killer outright. Dizas, called **the Mouse**, was a thraex of the school until Glaucus threw him out last winter for stealing from the infirmary. He runs knife-men out of a burned-out taberna off the Vicus Tuscus.

   Glaucus also offers the player a place on his sand as a paid guest. That is the side quest *The Oath* (`lud-01-sacramentum`): optional, and it does not take over the tracker.
5. **The Mouse.** In the burned taberna, Mus has Festus's satchel. The player can:
   - fight him and his two knife-men (he yields at the end);
   - frighten him into handing it over;
   - talk him into naming who paid: "a man with Syrian silver, at the Pepper Warehouses".

   The satchel holds an outer tablet with the address scraped off, and a **Parthian silver drachm**: Parthian money in a Roman killer's purse.
6. **Dusk.** At the strongrooms by lamplight Gratus breaks the seal. The message is in Festus's private cipher, and only his twin, Gemellus, can read it. Gemellus has been missing since the Ides of April.
   - Gratus: "Tonight is the Lemuria, the night the dead walk. His family will be up at midnight for the rite. Go to them. You promised him you'd tell his mother. And find me the brother."
   - He gives the player his token (it admits them to the Castra Peregrina later) and money for Festus's family.

## Chapter 3: Beans for the Dead (`mq-03-lemuria`)
*The Velabrum, the night of 11 May.*

1. **The Marii's house.** **Helpis**, Festus's mother, opens the door before the player knocks; she reads it in their face. **Fuscus**, his father, a veteran of the Flavian wars who now makes lamps, says nothing.
   - Helpis on Gemellus: gone since April.
   - Helpis on the cipher: the boys' game, "letters moved along so their father couldn't read their notes".
   - "Stay for the rite at midnight, and keep silent whatever you see."
2. **The rite** (Ovid, *Fasti* 5.429–44). At midnight Fuscus walks barefoot, washes his hands, and throws black beans behind him nine times without looking back: "These I send; with these beans I redeem me and mine." He clashes bronze and cries for the ghosts of his fathers to go out. In the dark, a figure in Festus's brown cloak crosses the end of the street and is gone.
3. **Clues** (two of three are enough):
   - the beans picked up one by one from the doorstep;
   - Festus's old brown cloak missing from its peg;
   - ink on the doorpost at a man's shoulder height. Helpis: "Gemellus always had ink on his hands. He copies books for Tryphon, by the statue of Vertumnus. He has a key to the back room."
4. **Gemellus** is in Tryphon's back room, wearing his brother's cloak. He's frightened and suspicious. With truth, persuasion or a threat he gives up the key: "Four. Every letter four along. We did it as boys."
5. **The message**, decoded:
   - IN DEDICATIONE ARCVS IN LOCO ALTO: "At the dedication, a bow on the high place."
   - EX ORIENTE PECVNIA PER HORREA PIPERATARIA: "Money from the East, through the Pepper Warehouses."
   - MONE GRATVM: "Warn Gratus."

   The player warns Gratus at the strongrooms.
6. **End of chapter 3.**
   - Gratus: "A bow on the high place. Tomorrow Caesar stands at the foot of his Column, and the high place is the Column itself. I'll have men on every roof. Be in the Forum of Trajan at dawn."
   - The journal closes the chapter. Chapter 4 begins at once.

## Chapter 4: One Hundred Feet (`mq-04-columna`)
*Forum of Trajan, 12 May AD 113, from first light.*

1. **Dawn.** The Forum is fuller than it was, and Trajan's daily walk is held. The Column's bronze door is sealed with lead.
   - **Objective:** **Be in the Forum of Trajan at first light.** Before 05:30 the journal says to wait (T) until then.
2. **Gratus's briefing.** Gratus stands in the Column court by the altar. He has moved his men onto the library roofs and into the gallery, and he has put the praetorians thirty paces deep round Caesar.
   - **Objective:** **Speak to Gratus in the Column court.**
   - Gratus: "Men on both library roofs and in the gallery." Apollodorus had the Column's door sealed with lead at first light, "after his men swept the stair." Nobody is up there but the statue.
   - Gratus on who else knows: "Pudens: he's my chief." Pudens had told him, "Then let them shoot, and we'll see who hands them the bow."
   - If the player asks what happens if the bow isn't on the Column: "Then I'm a fool on the wrong roof. The message said a high place. Look up. There's nothing higher in Rome today."
   - Gratus gives the post: "Stand at the Column's door. Nobody goes in or out. … If anything feels wrong, shout, and don't wait for my leave."
   - **Optional:** ask Apollodorus, the builder, about the door. He says the scaffold gang, Dacian captives, was in the stair before dawn taking down the last hoist ropes, and that his foreman sealed the door behind them. He assumes they all came out.
3. **The ceremony.**
   - **Objective:** **Take your post at the Column's door.** The ceremony starts when the player is at the door between 06:00 and 18:00. Earlier, Gratus says Caesar comes "at the second hour", and the journal says to wait.
   - Caesar's party comes out onto the back steps of the Basilica Ulpia. Horns sound. The herald calls "Favete linguis!" ("Keep holy silence!") and reads the dedication from the pedestal. Trajan pours the libation at the altar.
   - **Optional:** look at the seal on the door. The lead has been cut and pressed back together to look whole. Someone has been inside since dawn. Gratus sees the player looking and starts over. The arrow follows four seconds later. If the player never looks, Gratus walks over at about the time of the herald and the arrow comes when he is halfway there.
4. **The arrow.**
   - A figure stands up on the Column's platform: Bitus, small and high, with a bow. The arrow strikes Gratus in the shoulder and he falls.
   - The court panics. A woman screams, the praetorians hurry Caesar back into the basilica, and the crowd scatters. Crito, Caesar's physician, kneels by Gratus.
   - Gratus, from the ground: "On top. He's on the top. The seal… Go! Alive if you can. I want the hand that paid him."
   - The Column's door opens.
5. **The stair.**
   - **Objective:** **Climb the Column: stop the archer.** The player goes in through the door into a vestibule and a small chamber under the court, then up 185 steps of spiral stair lit by slit windows.
   - Two Dacian knife-men were left to hold the stair, one on each of the first two landings. They wait and fight when the player reaches them.
   - **Optional:** the empty chamber under the court. It is a square room cut into the pedestal, with a marble shelf and nothing on it. The workmen call it the tomb. Nobody says whose.
   - At the top a small bronze hatch opens onto the platform.
6. **The archer.** The platform is a marble slab round the drum that carries Caesar's gilded statue, about a hundred feet over the Forum.
   - Bitus starts on the far side of the drum, shoots up to six arrows (a shield blocks them), then fights with a sica. He yields at a quarter of his health.
   - **Objective:** **Stop the archer.**
   - Bitus, when asked: "Mucapor gave me the bow." The silver came from "a man with Syrian rings who smelled of pepper". Bitus, son of Dida, a captive from the hills above Sarmizegetusa, carried stone for this forum for six years. His target was "the centurion in the grey cloak, the one going about asking questions at the pepper house. Caesar, they said, is for later."
   - Bitus on why: "Your stone says we lost. I wanted it to say one more thing."
   - When he yields, the player chooses. **Spare** (or rob or arrest) and Bitus talks, then Pudens's men come up the stair and take him down. **Kill** and the body is searched: **optional objective "Search the archer"**.
   - Either way the bone token scratched with MVCAPOR goes to the player: Bitus gives it up when asked who paid, or it is on his body.
7. **Aftermath.**
   - **Objective:** **Go down to Gratus.** Crito, by Gratus, says the arrow missed the great vessel by a finger: "He'll live, if he lies still, which he won't." Crito gives the player the Dacian arrow that came out of Gratus.
   - **Objective:** **Speak to Pudens.** Pudens, chief of the couriers, is beside Gratus and Crito. He says: "Titus Aufidius Pudens. I keep the couriers, and the couriers keep the secrets." He reacts to Bitus's fate ("A live one talks" or, for a dead archer, "Dead men are poor company and worse witnesses"). He says Gratus will live, then gives the official story: "There was no arrow." A centurion was taken ill in the sun, and a hawk was seen over the Column. The player is to say it back.
   - Pudens summons the player: "Come to the Castra Peregrina tomorrow, on the Caelian. Show the guard Gratus's token." He gives the player a ring that opens doors.
   - **Rewards:** 80 denarii and skill XP in blades and athletics (the quest), and the couriers' ring and the Dacian arrow (given in conversation by Pudens and Crito).
8. **Done.**
   - Banner: "Act I continues" / **The Board**: "Pudens's offer, 13 May, comes in a future update."
   - The calendar runs on to 13 May at the next midnight.
   - Folk in the city carry a rumour about the omen at the Column.

## What comes next (designed, not built)
- **mq-05 The Board:** Pudens's offer at the Castra Peregrina, on the Caelian, on 13 May. He makes the player his deniable agent, and the conspiracy board opens (`GDD.md` §10.2).

## Side threads in reach of Act I
- ***The Oath*** (Ludus Magnus): sign on as a guest and fight three practice bouts, the last against Nereus. It's offered by Glaucus, and it's optional.
- ***Brawl at the Fountain***, ***Black Beans***, ***The Leaning Insula*** and ***What Venus Hides***: misc quests found by walking about.
- **Daily life** (no quests): the games in the Colosseum, the trades, the Ludus drills.
