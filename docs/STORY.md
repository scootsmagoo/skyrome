# Skyrome: the story (Act I as played)

This is the story as the player meets it. `docs/GDD.md` §10 is the design of the whole main quest, and `docs/CONTENT.md` §3 has the quest specs. Where this file and those two disagree, this file wins: it was written after a playtest found the opening murky ("why was I attacked? why am I fighting gladiators?").

## The rules this story keeps
1. **Every scene answers three questions.** Who wants something, why, and what the player should do next. Each character says it in plain words.
2. **Nothing happens at random in the opening.** The first fight is set up beforehand: Festus says he is being followed. The killer is seen, and he runs with something. Street muggers stay off the streets until Act I's opening night is over.
3. **One thread at a time.** The main quest never needs a side quest to go on. Side quests show up as offers and never take over the tracker.
4. **The next step is always on screen.**
   - The objective tracker and the gold marker point at the next step, and the marker rides on the person if it's a person.
   - A banner announces each new objective.
   - The journal tells the story in the first person, past tense.
5. **History holds** (AD 113, 11 May, the eve of the Column's dedication). Fictional people do fictional things in real places. Real people are seen but don't speak.

## Background (what is true before the game starts)
- **The war:** Trajan is about to go to war with Parthia. Couriers ride between Rome and the army gathering in Syria.
- **The couriers:** the *frumentarii*, soldiers seconded as imperial couriers and, quietly, as the emperor's informers. Their camp is the Castra Peregrina on the Caelian.
- **The plot:** someone in Rome is taking Parthian silver to make trouble at the dedication of Trajan's Column on 12 May.
- **Festus and his twin:**
  - A courier, **Gaius Marius Festus**, picked up a warning in Antioch. He is carrying it home in his own cipher, because he has learned that his camp leaks.
  - Only his twin brother, **Gemellus**, a copyist, knows the cipher (a game the boys played to hide their notes from their father).
  - Gemellus vanished in April after a client frightened him. He is hiding in the back room of Tryphon's bookshop.

## Chapter 1: The Dripping Gate (`mq-01-madida-capena`)
*Porta Capena, 11 May, 04:30.*

*How to reach it:* the plain game link now starts in the Forum at 10:00 with the opening skipped; this chapter plays from `?story=1` (or a new game from the title menu, `?menu=1`).

1. **The cart.** The player rides in on the last night wine-cart. The courier Festus shares the cart and opens the conversation by himself. He says:
   - he carries a sealed dispatch for his centurion;
   - two riders have kept pace with the cart since Bovillae, never passing and never falling back;
   - he'd like company through the gate: "Two are harder to knife than one. There's a denarius in it."

   **Objective:** Walk through the Capena Gate with Festus. He walks beside the player.
2. **The ambush.** Under the dripping arch three men step out. A hooded man with a curved blade knifes Festus and runs off up the Circus valley with his satchel. The other two turn on the player ("Get the tablet! It's not in the bag!"). The tutorial hints for fighting play here.
   **Objective:** Fight off the attackers.
3. **Festus dying.** He presses the sealed tablet into the player's hand.
   - "They knew my road and my hour. Only the camp knew that. Don't take it to the camp."
   - "Take it to Gratus, my centurion. He keeps an office in the strongrooms under the Temple of Castor, in the Forum. Only Gratus."
   - "The one with the curved blade took my satchel. Up from under, like a gladiator."
   - "Tell my mother. The Marii, lamp-makers, in the Velabrum."

   **Objectives:** Hear Festus's last words. Optional: ask the carter Dromo what he saw (the hooded man walked like a fighter and ran toward the Circus).
4. **Chapter complete.** Chapter 2 begins at once.

## Chapter 2: The Sealed Tablet (`mq-02-tabella`)
*The Forum, the Ludus Magnus and the Vicus Tuscus. 11 May, from dawn to dusk.*

1. **The road.** Take the tablet to Gratus. The marker leads up the Circus valley, through the Velabrum and the Vicus Tuscus, to the Temple of Castor in the Forum. *Optional:* search Festus's body (his purse and an unsent letter home).
2. **The keeper.** The temple is shut for the Lemuria, but the strongrooms in its podium are not. Their keeper, **Chrysippus**, doesn't give out his clients' names. Showing him Festus's seal (a horseman with a spear: the couriers' mark) sends him running for Gratus. A bribe or a good word works too.
3. **Gratus** (centurion of the frumentarii) hears how Festus died and lays it out:
   - "Festus's road and hour were known only in our camp. Someone there sold him."
   - "If I'm seen taking that tablet in the Forum in daylight, whoever paid for Festus knows it reached me. Keep it on you, and bring it after sunset, when the Forum's empty."
   - "Meanwhile, the man who did it. A curved blade, up from under: that's how a thraex finishes a man on his knees. Gladiators. Go to the Ludus Magnus by the Amphitheatre and ask for Glaucus, their chief trainer. He knows every man who ever held a sica in Rome. Get me a name."
   - Why the player? "Because nobody knows your face. And because Festus chose you."
4. **Glaucus** at the Ludus names the killer outright. Dizas, called **the Mouse**, was a thraex of the school until Glaucus threw him out last winter for stealing from the infirmary. He runs knife-men out of a burned-out taberna off the Vicus Tuscus.

   Glaucus also offers the player a place on his sand as a paid guest. That is the side quest *The Oath* (`lud-01-sacramentum`): optional, and it does not take over the tracker.
5. **The Mouse.** In the burned taberna, Mus has Festus's satchel. The player can:
   - fight him and his two knife-men (he yields at the end);
   - frighten him into handing it over;
   - talk him into naming who paid: "a man with Syrian silver, at the Pepper Warehouses".

   The satchel holds an outer tablet with the address scraped off, and a **Parthian silver drachm**: Parthian money in a Roman killer's purse.
6. **Dusk.** At the strongrooms by lamplight Gratus breaks the seal. The message is in Festus's private cipher, and only his twin, Gemellus, can read it. Gemellus has been missing since the Ides of April.
   - Gratus: "Tonight is the Lemuria, the night the dead walk. His family will be up at midnight for the rite. Go to them. You promised him you'd tell his mother. And find me the brother."
   - He gives the player his token (it admits them to the Castra Peregrina later) and money for Festus's family.

## Chapter 3: Beans for the Dead (`mq-03-lemuria`)
*The Velabrum, the night of 11 May.*

1. **The Marii's house.** **Helpis**, Festus's mother, opens the door before the player knocks; she reads it in their face. **Fuscus**, his father, a veteran of the Flavian wars who now makes lamps, says nothing.
   - Helpis on Gemellus: gone since April.
   - Helpis on the cipher: the boys' game, "letters moved along so their father couldn't read their notes".
   - "Stay for the rite at midnight, and keep silent whatever you see."
2. **The rite** (Ovid, *Fasti* 5.429–44). At midnight Fuscus walks barefoot, washes his hands, and throws black beans behind him nine times without looking back: "These I send; with these beans I redeem me and mine." He clashes bronze and cries for the ghosts of his fathers to go out. In the dark, a figure in Festus's brown cloak crosses the end of the street and is gone.
3. **Clues** (two of three are enough):
   - the beans picked up one by one from the doorstep;
   - Festus's old brown cloak missing from its peg;
   - ink on the doorpost at a man's shoulder height. Helpis: "Gemellus always had ink on his hands. He copies books for Tryphon, by the statue of Vertumnus. He has a key to the back room."
4. **Gemellus** is in Tryphon's back room, wearing his brother's cloak. He's frightened and suspicious. With truth, persuasion or a threat he gives up the key: "Four. Every letter four along. We did it as boys."
5. **The message**, decoded:
   - IN DEDICATIONE ARCVS IN LOCO ALTO: "At the dedication, a bow on the high place."
   - EX ORIENTE PECVNIA PER HORREA PIPERATARIA: "Money from the East, through the Pepper Warehouses."
   - MONE GRATVM: "Warn Gratus."

   The player warns Gratus at the strongrooms.
6. **End of chapter 3.**
   - Gratus: "A bow on the high place. Tomorrow Caesar stands at the foot of his Column, and the high place is the Column itself. I'll have men on every roof. Be in the Forum of Trajan at dawn."
   - The journal closes the chapter. Chapter 4 begins at once.

## Chapter 4: One Hundred Feet (`mq-04-columna`)
*Forum of Trajan, 12 May AD 113, from first light.*

1. **Dawn.** The Forum is fuller than it was, and Trajan's daily walk is held. The Column's bronze door is sealed with lead.
   - **Objective:** **Be in the Forum of Trajan at first light.** Before 05:30 the journal says to wait (T) until then.
2. **Gratus's briefing.** Gratus stands in the Column court by the altar. He has moved his men onto the library roofs and into the gallery, and he has put the praetorians thirty paces deep round Caesar.
   - **Objective:** **Speak to Gratus in the Column court.**
   - Gratus: "Men on both library roofs and in the gallery." Apollodorus had the Column's door sealed with lead at first light, "after his men swept the stair." Nobody is up there but the statue.
   - Gratus on who else knows: "Pudens: he's my chief." Pudens had told him, "Then let them shoot, and we'll see who hands them the bow."
   - If the player asks what happens if the bow isn't on the Column: "Then I'm a fool on the wrong roof. The message said a high place. Look up. There's nothing higher in Rome today."
   - Gratus gives the post: "Stand at the Column's door. Nobody goes in or out. … If anything feels wrong, shout, and don't wait for my leave."
   - **Optional:** ask Apollodorus, the builder, about the door. He says the scaffold gang, Dacian captives, was in the stair before dawn taking down the last hoist ropes, and that his foreman sealed the door behind them. He assumes they all came out.
3. **The ceremony.**
   - **Objective:** **Take your post at the Column's door.** The ceremony starts when the player is at the door between 06:00 and 18:00. Earlier, Gratus says Caesar comes "at the second hour", and the journal says to wait.
   - Caesar's party comes out onto the back steps of the Basilica Ulpia. Horns sound. The herald calls "Favete linguis!" ("Keep holy silence!") and reads the dedication from the pedestal. Trajan pours the libation at the altar.
   - **Optional:** look at the seal on the door. The lead has been cut and pressed back together to look whole. Someone has been inside since dawn. Gratus sees the player looking and starts over. The arrow follows four seconds later. If the player never looks, Gratus walks over at about the time of the herald and the arrow comes when he is halfway there.
4. **The arrow.**
   - A figure stands up on the Column's platform: Bitus, small and high, with a bow. The arrow strikes Gratus in the shoulder and he falls.
   - The court panics. A woman screams, the praetorians hurry Caesar back into the basilica, and the crowd scatters. Crito, Caesar's physician, kneels by Gratus.
   - Gratus, from the ground: "On top. He's on the top. The seal… Go! Alive if you can. I want the hand that paid him."
   - The Column's door opens.
5. **The stair.**
   - **Objective:** **Climb the Column: stop the archer.** The player goes in through the door into a vestibule and a small chamber under the court, then up 185 steps of spiral stair lit by slit windows.
   - Two Dacian knife-men were left to hold the stair, one on each of the first two landings. They wait and fight when the player reaches them.
   - **Optional:** the empty chamber under the court. It is a square room cut into the pedestal, with a marble shelf and nothing on it. The workmen call it the tomb. Nobody says whose.
   - At the top a small bronze hatch opens onto the platform.
6. **The archer.** The platform is a marble slab round the drum that carries Caesar's gilded statue, about a hundred feet over the Forum.
   - Bitus starts on the far side of the drum, shoots up to six arrows (a shield blocks them), then fights with a sica. He yields at a quarter of his health.
   - **Objective:** **Stop the archer.**
   - Bitus, when asked: "Mucapor gave me the bow." The silver came from "a man with Syrian rings who smelled of pepper". Bitus, son of Dida, a captive from the hills above Sarmizegetusa, carried stone for this forum for six years. His target was "the centurion in the grey cloak, the one going about asking questions at the pepper house. Caesar, they said, is for later."
   - Bitus on why: "Your stone says we lost. I wanted it to say one more thing."
   - When he yields, the player chooses. **Spare** (or rob or arrest) and Bitus talks, then Pudens's men come up the stair and take him down. **Kill** and the body is searched: **optional objective "Search the archer"**.
   - Either way the bone token scratched with MVCAPOR goes to the player: Bitus gives it up when asked who paid, or it is on his body.
7. **Aftermath.**
   - **Objective:** **Go down to Gratus.** Crito, by Gratus, says the arrow missed the great vessel by a finger: "He'll live, if he lies still, which he won't." Crito gives the player the Dacian arrow that came out of Gratus.
   - **Objective:** **Speak to Pudens.** Pudens, chief of the couriers, is beside Gratus and Crito. He says: "Titus Aufidius Pudens. I keep the couriers, and the couriers keep the secrets." He reacts to Bitus's fate ("A live one talks" or, for a dead archer, "Dead men are poor company and worse witnesses"). He says Gratus will live, then gives the official story: "There was no arrow." A centurion was taken ill in the sun, and a hawk was seen over the Column. The player is to say it back.
   - Pudens summons the player: "Come to the Castra Peregrina tomorrow, on the Caelian. Show the guard Gratus's token." He gives the player a ring that opens doors.
   - **Rewards:** 80 denarii and skill XP in blades and athletics (the quest), and the couriers' ring and the Dacian arrow (given in conversation by Pudens and Crito).
8. **Done.**
   - Banner: "Act I continues" / **The Board**: "Pudens waits at the Castra Peregrina, on the Caelian, on 13 May." Chapter 5 begins at once.
   - The calendar runs on to 13 May at the next midnight.
   - Folk in the city carry a rumour about the omen at the Column.

## Chapter 5: The Board (`mq-05-tabula`)
*The Caelian, 13 May AD 113, the day after the Column.*

*How to reach it:* play on from Chapter 4, or start from `?part=camp` (the morning after, by the Colosseum) or `?part=board` (inside the camp, at Pudens's door).

**What this chapter does.** It answers the player's big question after the Column: *what was all that, and what happens now?* Pudens explains it in plain words. The arrow was a rehearsal: the real blow is meant for Caesar when he leaves for the East in the autumn. The couriers' camp leaks, so Pudens can't use his own men. He hires the player as his outsider and shows them the board: four groups of plotters, three faces in each, and the clues the player already carries pinned under them. Act I ends there.

**What the player already has** (from Chapters 2–4), and where it ends up on the board:

| Clue | From | Points to |
|---|---|---|
| The scraped wax tablet with three half-names | Mus's chest (Ch. 2) | **The Purse** |
| The Parthian silver drachm | Mus's chest (Ch. 2) | **The Eastern Gold** |
| "A man with Syrian rings who smelled of pepper" | Mus (Ch. 2) and Bitus (Ch. 4) | **The Eastern Gold** |
| The bone token scratched MVCAPOR | Bitus (Ch. 4) | **The Dacian Blades** |

1. **The summons.** The chapter starts as soon as Chapter 4 ends, on 12 May. The calendar steps on to 13 May at the next midnight.
   - **Objective:** **Go to the Castra Peregrina on the Caelian.** The marker leads past the Colosseum, up the slope of the Caelian, and under the Arch of Dolabella, where Nero's aqueduct crosses the street. The camp's gate is just beyond.
   - The journal: "He had named the place out loud, in a forum full of people. Anyone could have heard him."
   - Pudens said *tomorrow*, and the camp sees visitors from first light on 13 May until dark. Come too early (still 12 May) and the gate guard says, "The chief's still in the Forum, cleaning up after the Column. Come back tomorrow, at first light." At night he says, "The chief sees nobody after dark. Come at first light." A hint says to wait (T).
2. **The follower.** This fight is set up before it starts.
   - As the player nears the camp (by the Arch of Dolabella, or from any other side), a water-carrier stops them: "Friend, the man in the dark cloak has been walking where you walk since the Colosseum. Mind yourself under the arch."
   - **Objective:** **You are being followed.** The marker rides on the man in the dark cloak, a sicarius, a dozen paces behind.
   - He closes in and attacks. His blade is poisoned: a cut keeps hurting for a few seconds. He fights to the death.
   - **Objective:** **Search the man in the dark cloak.** He carries a few coins, his knife and a small **wax tablet**. Written on it: "THE NEW ONE. CAELIAN. BEFORE THE CAMP." The wax is red, and there's a horseman with a spear stamped on the frame: the couriers' own tablets.
3. **The gate.**
   - **Objective:** **Get into the camp: show the guard Gratus's token.** A soldier on the gate stops the player. Showing Gratus's token or Pudens's ring opens the gate. A player who has lost both can still talk their way in, but it's harder without the token. (A player who just walks past him into the principia is in too.)
   - The guard calls a courier, **Mardonius**, who is lounging by the gate. He is friendly and talkative, and asks what the archer said on the Column. The player can tell him or not. Then he points the way: Pudens's office, across the court, the door on the right. (He is one of the plotters, in the Eastern Gold. Nothing in this chapter says so. In Act II, a player who told him finds the plotters knew it.)
4. **Gratus** *(optional).* He's in the camp's infirmary, off the principia's court, already up, with his arm strapped to his chest.
   - **Objective (optional):** **Visit Gratus in the infirmary.**
   - Gratus: "Crito said lie still. Crito isn't the one they shot."
   - On what Pudens wants: "He'll offer you work, and he'll make it sound like a favour. It isn't one. Take it anyway: you're in this already, and it's safer to be in it with him."
   - On the camp: "Festus's road was sold from inside these walls. I still don't know by whom. Don't tell anyone here more than you tell me."
5. **Pudens.** He's in the commander's office off the principia's court, with a bowl of figs on the table.
   - **Objective:** **Speak to Pudens.**
   - **The tablet** (if the player shows him the sicarius's tablet): Pudens turns it over and goes quiet. "That's my wax. Somebody in this camp sent that knife after you, and wrote it on my own tablets." That's why he needs the player: "My men talk to each other. You talk to nobody. Nobody knows your face, and the ones who do, you just killed." If the player didn't search the sicarius, Pudens says the same thing about Festus instead: "Festus's road was known only in this camp, and someone sold it."
   - **What the arrow was:** "Your Dacian told you himself. The centurion was for now; Caesar is for later. 'Later' means October, when Caesar rides out for the East and all of Rome lines the road to watch him go. That's when they'll try."
   - **Who "they" are:** "Not one man. Money from the East, men with grudges, and someone at the top who wants something money can't buy. The kind of man who wears a toga."
   - **The offer:** "I want you to work for me. Not on my rolls: you're not a soldier and not a courier. If you're caught, I've never met you. I pay well, and I pay on time." He never says *spy*. Asked outright, he says: "Couriers carry letters. Sometimes they read them first."
   - The player can ask more before answering:
     - "Why not tell Caesar?" → "And tell him what? That I can't keep my own camp clean? Bring me names, then we tell him."
     - "What's in it for me?" → money now, and "a friend in the one office in Rome that hears everything".
     - "Not yet" → "Take your time. Rome isn't going anywhere, and neither are they." The quest waits. Speaking to him again picks it up.
   - **Objective:** **Accept Pudens's offer** (it appears once he has made it).
6. **The board.** Pudens unlocks a chest and spreads its wax tablets across the table: the board. Nobody else in the camp has seen it.
   - **Objective:** **Look at the board** (on his table: press E). The journal opens at its new **Board** page. It lists the four groups of plotters, and each has three faces, all hidden. Under each are the clues pinned to it and Pudens's word on where to start:
     - **The Purse:** the money men. Pinned under it, the scraped tablet. Pudens: "Half a name isn't a name. But '…NARIVS AVIT…' buys grain for the army, and a man who sells grain for a war wants the war."
     - **The Eastern Gold:** where the Parthian silver comes in. Pinned under it, the drachm and "Syrian rings, smelled of pepper". Pudens: "The Pepper Warehouses, on the Sacred Way. Gratus was asking questions there. That's why they shot him."
     - **The Dacian Blades:** the knives. Pinned under it, the MVCAPOR token. Pudens: "Mucapor. A fighter's name. Ask at the Dacian school, by the Colosseum."
       - If the player spared Bitus, the board says he is in the cells, and Pudens says so when asked: "Your archer is in my cell, across the court. He's talking. Go and see him, if you want." *(optional objective)* Bitus tells the player where Mucapor trains and that he "laughs with his whole face, and fights with a sickle as long as your arm".
     - **The Toga:** empty. Pudens: "Someone knew where Caesar would stand and where the praetorians would be. Someone high. I've nothing on him yet."
   - Below the four groups: **In the camp**: the leak, and the knife-man's tablet.
   - Pudens sums it up: "Four groups, three men in each. Unmask enough of them to break three groups, and we'll know enough to stop October. Start wherever you like."
7. **Done.**
   - **Rewards:** 100 denarii (Pudens's advance) and skill XP in rhetoric.
   - The board stays in the journal (J, then the Board page). Opening it shows what the player knows, any time.
   - A spared Bitus stays in the cell.
   - Banner: "End of Act I" / **The Board**: "Act II comes in a future update."
   - The calendar runs on freely from here. Nothing waits for a date until Act II.

## Chapter 6: The Purse (`mq-06-sacculus`)
*The Caelian, the Sacred Way, the Basilica Aemilia and the Vicus Tuscus. From 13 May AD 113, at the player's own pace.*

*Status: written for review, not built.*

**What this chapter does.** It is the first group on Pudens's board: the money behind the plot.
- The scraped tablet's three half-names are three real men.
  - **Sextus Pinarius Avitus** has the contract to feed the army bound for the East.
  - **Tiberius Claudius Euhodus**, a freedman, sells arms on the Sacred Way.
  - **Hermogenes** is a banker at a table in the shops of the Basilica Aemilia.
- The player proves, one man at a time, how Parthian silver becomes Roman coin, then knives, then grain sacks with the knives inside.
- Every man has two ways to prove him.
- The chapter ends face to face with Avitus, and the player decides what happens to him.
- Among his papers is the first hint of something bigger: a scribe paid "for the fine hand".

**How the money moves** (what the player works out):
1. Hermogenes changes the Parthian silver into Roman coin, by weight, with no names in his books.
2. With that coin, Euhodus buys blades: sicae for the Dacian knife-men, and bows.
3. Euhodus sends the blades to Avitus in sealed grain sacks. They sit in the warehouse bays Avitus rents at the Horrea Agrippiana, among sacks of the army's wheat. Nobody searches the army's wheat.
4. Avitus borrowed a fortune from Hermogenes to buy up last year's wheat for the war. The men who pay him have promised him that the next emperor will pay his price and forget his debts.

**What the player already has**, and where it is used:

| Clue | From | Used here |
|---|---|---|
| The scraped tablet ("…NARIVS AVIT…", "…VHOD…", "HERMOG…") | Mus's chest (Ch. 2) | Three names to find |
| The Parthian silver drachm | Mus's chest (Ch. 2). Gratus kept it as evidence; Pudens hands it back at the start of this chapter | One way to prove Hermogenes |
| The Dacian arrow (from Gratus's shoulder) | Crito (Ch. 4) | One way to prove Euhodus |
| Told Mardonius what the archer said | Ch. 5 choice | A warning line before the last fight |

**The board.**
- Each man proven is one face unmasked (flag `board:<id>`). When all three are unmasked, **The Purse** is broken.
- Proving a man is not arresting him; Pudens decides who is arrested (step 6).

1. **The lead.** Chapter 5 now ends with the banner "Act II: The Board" ("Start wherever you like"). Chapter 6 starts when the player picks this group, in either of two ways:
   - **Ask Pudens about the Purse.** He's in his office in the camp, by day.
     - Pudens: "The money men. Three half-names on a scraped tablet. Start with the grain: '…NARIVS AVIT…' buys wheat for the army, and a man who sells wheat for a war wants the war. Ask at the Horrea Agrippiana, on the Vicus Tuscus. Every big buyer rents a bay there."
     - He hands back the drachm: "Gratus kept this as evidence. Take it. A banker will tell you more about a coin than I can."
   - **Or just go to the Horrea Agrippiana** and ask the warehouse keeper about Pinarius (the drachm is then given by Pudens whenever the player next sees him).
   - **Objective:** **Find out who "…NARIVS AVIT…" is.** The marker points to the Horrea Agrippiana.

2. **The warehouse keeper.** **Primigenius**, the *horrearius* (keeper of the warehouse), sits at the gate with his tally tablets.
   - Most of the warehouse is cloth dealers; one private buyer rents the back bays.
   - Primigenius says it straight out:
     - "Pinarius? Sextus Pinarius Avitus. He has the contract to feed the army for the Syrian war. He bought up all the old wheat he could find this spring, and the price doubled."
     - "He rents three bays at the back and keeps them sealed. His own porters do the carrying, not mine. I don't like a sealed bay."
   - Asked who comes to see Avitus: "Two men, regular as the Nones. Euhodus, who sells arms on the Sacred Way: his sign's by the Meta Sudans. And a banker, Hermogenes, from the shops at the Basilica Aemilia."
   - That accounts for all three half-names. **Objective (done):** the names. Three new objectives appear, in any order:
     - **Prove what Euhodus sells, and to whom.**
     - **Prove what Hermogenes changes, and for whom.**
     - **Find out what Avitus keeps in his sealed bays.**

3. **Euhodus, the arms dealer** (his shop on the Sacred Way, by the Meta Sudans).
   - He sells the player swords like anyone else; he is friendly and in a hurry. He has hinted before: it was "a very good spring for the contractors… Don't write that down."
   - **Two ways to prove him:**
     - **The arrow.** Show him the Dacian arrow that came out of Gratus.
       - The red bands on the shaft are Dacian work, but the grey goose fletching is his own: "That's my fletching. I sold three hundred of those."
       - With a Rhetoric check he gives up the buyer to save himself: "Pinarius took them. Bows, arrows, two hundred sicae, sent in grain sacks. Paid in good coin, changed at Hermogenes' table: his stamp was on the bags."
       - Then: "I sell knives. I don't ask whose throat."
     - **The shop boy.** Euhodus never leaves his shop (he sleeps over it), so his strongbox can't be got at.
       - His shop boy **Felix** can bring out the ledger while his master is at the forge in the back. Felix wants coin, or a good reason (Rhetoric): "He beats me anyway."
       - The ledger has the order: "To P. A., Horrea Agrippiana, in grain sacks: 200 sicae, 6 bows, 300 arrows. Paid through H."
   - Either way: **Euhodus is unmasked.** The journal notes how.

4. **Hermogenes, the banker** (his table between the barber and the physician, in the shops of the Basilica Aemilia).
   - He is polite, careful and very tired. His clerk **Lycus**, a young man with a stylus behind his ear, does the counting.
   - **Two ways to prove him:**
     - **The drachm.** Ask Hermogenes to change the Parthian drachm.
       - He doesn't blink: "Parthian. One denarius and a quarter, by weight, as for the others."
       - A Mercatura check, or simply asking "What others?", catches it: he has a ready price for Parthian silver, so he has changed a great deal of it.
       - He shuts his table and asks the player to leave. That's proof enough.
     - **Lycus.** Lycus is honest and frightened, and doesn't know what the money is for.
       - With a kind word (Rhetoric) or a few coins, he shows the player his wax tablets: "Eastern silver, by weight, for Pinarius. Every Nones since March. My master says don't write the names. I write them small."
   - Either way: **Hermogenes is unmasked.**

5. **Avitus's sealed bays** (the back of the Horrea Agrippiana). **Two ways in:**
   - **Primigenius opens a bay.** Once the player has proved Euhodus or Hermogenes, showing Primigenius the proof is enough for him to break a seal "for the army's sake". He opens one bay by day.
   - **The night porter.** At night a few coins buy the night porter's back, and the player cuts a seal.
   - Inside, among the sacks of wheat, three sacks are heavier than grain. In them are sicae wrapped in sacking, bows and arrows.
   - **Avitus is unmasked.**

6. **Avitus.** When all three men are unmasked, a new objective appears: **Face Avitus.** He is in his counting-room, a door off the court beside the bays.
   - **The warning, before the fight.**
     - Whoever opened the bay (Primigenius by day, the porter by night) says: "He'll know a seal's been cut by now. He keeps two hired knives in his counting-room, men of the Mouse's sort."
     - If the player told Mardonius what the archer said in Chapter 5, they add: "And a man came from the Caelian yesterday, asking what you look like." (This hints at the leak; it doesn't name him.)
   - **Avitus talks first.** He is fat, clever and frightened, and he doesn't deny anything. He wants the player to understand:
     - "A war is the best customer Rome has. I bought the wheat for it, every modius I could find, with Hermogenes' money. If the war goes the way Caesar wants, I'm rich. If it's slow, I'm ruined."
     - "Then men came who promised me better: a new emperor who pays my price, and a debt that disappears. I didn't ask who. I asked how much."
     - "I don't hold a knife. I hold a warehouse."
   - **The offer:** 300 denarii to walk away and let him leave Rome tonight.
   - **The fight is set off by the answer.** If the player refuses the money or says the word "arrest", Avitus calls his two knife-men from the back room ("Show our friend out."). They fight to the death; Avitus doesn't fight.
     - If the player takes the money, there is no fight: Avitus leaves (see "Let him run").
   - **The choice**, once the knife-men are down:
     - **Arrest him for Pudens.** He goes quietly. Pudens's couriers take him to the cells at the Castra Peregrina. In later chapters he is a witness: he knows a man in the Toga.
     - **Let him run** (with or without the 300 denarii). He's gone down the Tiber by night boat to Ostia. He warns the plotters, and the Toga's chapter is harder.
     - **Kill him.** Murder in the city is a crime, as anywhere.
   - **His papers.** The chest in his counting-room holds:
     - the army contract;
     - Hermogenes' loans;
     - one line that doesn't fit: "To M. Ulpius Chrysogonus, of the chancery: 2,000 sesterces, for the fine hand."

     Nobody here explains the last line. It is pinned on the board under **The Toga**: "A scribe of the chancery, paid by a grain man for fine work. Fine work at what?"

7. **Pudens** (the Castra Peregrina, by day; at night a hint says to wait until morning).
   - **Objective:** **Report to Pudens.** He hears it all and eats a fig.
   - **On Euhodus and Hermogenes:** "We leave them where they are. They know Avitus, and nobody above him. Arrest them, and the men at the top know we're close. A watched man is worth more than a jailed one." Both keep trading, and the player can still buy from them.
   - **On Avitus:**
     - Arrested: "Good. He's the one who knows the men above. He'll talk; fat men do."
     - Ran: "Then he's warning them now. Next time, bring me the man, not his money."
     - Dead: "A dead man can't name his friends. Let's hope his papers can."
   - **On the scribe:** "Chrysogonus. A chancery man. Why does a wheat dealer pay a scribe for his handwriting? I don't like that at all."
   - **Rewards:**
     - 150 denarii;
     - skill XP in Mercatura, plus Rhetoric for the ways the player talked;
     - the board marks **The Purse: broken**.

## Chapter 7: The Eastern Gold (`mq-07-aurum-parthicum`)
*The Sacred Way, the Circus Maximus, the Subura and the Caelian. From 13 May AD 113, at the player's own pace.*

*Status: built (`?part=gold` starts it at Pudens's office). Decided with the owner: no initiation into the cult in this version; Gemellus reads the cipher; Mardonius's three fates stay.*

**What this chapter does.** It is the second group on Pudens's board: where the Parthian silver comes from, and who in the couriers' camp is selling their secrets.
- Three men, as on the board:
  - **Malchus son of Iarhai, of Palmyra**, a wholesaler of pepper and silk at the Horrea Piperataria (the Pepper Warehouses, on the Sacred Way). He is "the man with Syrian rings who smelled of pepper" from Chapters 2 and 4.
  - **Asclepiodotus the Chaldaean**, an astrologer from Seleucia who reads the stars for great houses.
  - **Mardonius**, the friendly courier at the camp gate. He is the leak.
- The player proves each man two ways, as in Chapter 6.
- The chapter ends under the Subura, in a cave of the god Mithras, where the three meet. The player decides what happens to Mardonius.
- It answers a question from Act I: who sold Festus's road.

**How the gold moves** (what the player works out):
1. Parthian silver comes west with the caravans to Palmyra. Malchus's agents pack it into the bottom of pepper sacks. The customs men weigh pepper; nobody digs to the bottom of a pepper sack.
2. In Rome, Malchus sells the silver by weight to Hermogenes (Chapter 6: "as for the others").
3. Orders from the East travel with the silver, written as star tables. Only an astrologer has a reason to own them, and only Asclepiodotus can read them. He passes them on.
4. Mardonius tells them what the couriers know. He sold Festus's road in March. They meet where no one asks questions: at the night feasts of a Mithras cave, where the members are soldiers and nobody gives his real name.

**What the player already has**, and where it is used:

| Clue | From | Used here |
|---|---|---|
| "A man with Syrian rings who smelled of pepper" | Mus (Ch. 2) and Bitus (Ch. 4) | Leads to Malchus |
| The Parthian silver drachm | Ch. 2, given back in Ch. 6 | Malchus knows it at sight |
| Told Mardonius what the archer said | Ch. 5 choice | Mardonius sets a trap (step 6) |
| Hermogenes unmasked (Ch. 6, if played) | Ch. 6 | Lycus's tablets name "the Palmyrene" |
| Gemellus, Festus's twin, reads ciphers | Ch. 3 | He reads the star tables |

**The board.** Each man proven is one face unmasked; all three break **The Eastern Gold**.

1. **The lead.** The player picks this group:
   - **Ask Pudens about the Eastern Gold.** "The silver, and the pepper. Gratus asked questions at the Pepper Warehouses and took an arrow for it a week later. Somebody there didn't like his questions. Go and smell some pepper." He adds, quietly: "And mind what you say in this camp. Someone here talks."
   - **Or walk into the Horrea Piperataria** with the drachm and ask about Syrian silver.
   - **Objective:** **Find the man with Syrian rings.** The marker points to the Horrea Piperataria.

2. **The Pepper Warehouses.** The air is thick with pepper, cinnamon and nard. The warehouse clerk points the player to the man everyone knows:
   - "Rings on every finger? That's Malchus, the Palmyrene. Pepper by the shipload, silk by the bolt. He's the only one here who never haggles."
   - **Malchus** is charming, generous, and a little too pleased to see a stranger: "A friend of a friend? Everyone in Rome is a friend of a friend." He offers the player a cup of spiced wine and a job carrying a parcel, which is his way of finding out what the player wants.
   - **Objective:** **Prove what Malchus brings in under the pepper.** Two more appear once the player has met him, in any order:
     - **Find the Chaldaean who reads Malchus's letters.** Malchus mentions him without meaning to: "My stars? A man from Seleucia reads them. He's the only one in Rome who can."
     - **Find out who in the couriers' camp is talking.** The Lycus tablets (Ch. 6) or Malchus's ledger (below) show payments "to the Lion". Nobody knows yet who that is.

3. **Malchus, the merchant.** Two ways to prove him:
   - **The pepper (Mercatura).** Look at his prices. With a Mercatura check the player sees he sells pepper below what it costs in Alexandria: "A man who sells at a loss makes his money somewhere else." Then cut open one of his sacks in his storeroom bay (by day with the clerk distracted, or at night). Under a hand's depth of pepper there are drachms, the same silver as the player's.
   - **His strongbox (Locks & Seals).** His storeroom bay has a locked door and a strongbox (the lock-picking from the buildings update). At night, pick both. Inside: a bag of Parthian drachms and his ledger. The ledger is in Greek: "To H. (Hermogenes), silver by weight. To the Lion, 500, for the road in March."
     - Picking a lock while someone watches is a crime, as anywhere.
   - Either way: **Malchus is unmasked.**

4. **Asclepiodotus, the Chaldaean.** He reads for great houses, by appointment, in a fine upper flat on the Clivus Victoriae (the Palatine slope). Two ways to find him and prove him:
   - **Zenon.** The astrologer Zenon at the Circus arcade does the copying for other astrologers. With Rhetoric or Intimidation he admits it: "I copy tables for a Chaldaean from Seleucia. He pays in Syrian silver. And one of the nativities I copied was… Caesar's." Casting the emperor's stars is treason. Zenon gives the player the address and a copy of the strangest table.
   - **Buy a reading.** Go to Asclepiodotus as a client: 50 denarii, or Rhetoric to be seen without a patron's letter. While he reads the player's stars he is called out of the room for a moment. On his table lies a nativity of Caesar, with the month of October marked and one word in Greek: "the departure". The player can take it (a crime if seen) or remember it.
   - **The cipher.** The star tables are not about stars. Either table, the copy or the original, goes to **Gemellus** at Tryphon's bookshop. Festus's twin reads ciphers for pleasure. "Planets for letters. Schoolboys' trick, very old. It says: *October. The courier will say which gate.*"
   - **Asclepiodotus is unmasked** once the player has the table and Gemellus has read it.

5. **Mardonius, the courier** ("the Lion"). Two ways to prove him:
   - **The cave of Mithras.** Asked who "the Lion" in Malchus's ledger might be, Pudens explains: "That's a grade in the Mithras caves… Half my couriers go down to one under the Subura, off the Vicus Patricius. The Father of that cave is an old centurion, Cassius Ursus. Ask him who his Lions are."
     - The player finds the **Mithraeum**: a cave fitted into the cellar of an insula off the Vicus Patricius, behind a plain door with a raven scratched on it. Its keeper is the **Father** of the cave, **Cassius Ursus**, an old centurion and an honest man.
     - The Father is suspicious of strangers. With Rhetoric, or by showing Pudens's ring, he talks. He is angry: "Mardonius brought guests to the god's table: a Syrian with rings and a Chaldaean. Strangers. Uninitiated. And now I hear men in the Forum calling Mithras a Persian god, a god for traitors." The cave is being used, and the cult will be blamed.
     - He tells the player when the next feast is (the next night) and promises to let them in.
   - **His locker (Locks & Seals).** In the camp's barracks, Mardonius keeps a locked box. At night, pick it. Inside: a letter in the planet cipher (Gemellus reads it: "Festus comes by the Appian Way on the eleventh, at dawn, to the Capena gate") and a purse of drachms. This is proof that he sold Festus's road.
   - Either way: **Mardonius is unmasked.**

6. **The feast.** When all three are unmasked, the objective is: **Go down to the cave of Mithras at night.**
   - **If the player told Mardonius what the archer said in Chapter 5,** Mardonius knows they are close. Two hired knives wait on the stair down to the cave. They fight to the death. The Father: "He's sent men to my door. To the god's door."
   - **The cave.** A long, low vault with benches on both sides, lamps, and at the far end a painted relief of Mithras killing the bull. Mardonius, Malchus and Asclepiodotus sit apart from the soldiers.
   - **Mardonius talks first.** He is not ashamed: "Fifteen years carrying other men's letters. Antioch, Alexandria, Rome. Who thanked me? The man who pays for the war has never ridden in one. The men in the East pay better, and they pay on time." And about Festus: "I sold a road. I didn't hold the knife."
   - **The soldiers turn.** The Father stands up. The initiates are soldiers too, and they are on the player's side. Mardonius draws a knife.
   - **The fight.** Mardonius fights well, and yields when beaten. Malchus and Asclepiodotus stay where they are: the soldiers hold them.
   - **The choice**, when Mardonius yields:
     - **Take him to Pudens.** The leak, alive. He knows the name of the praetorian who gave the Column's plan, the first face of **The Toga** (a clue on the board).
     - **Leave him to the Father.** The cult judges its own. He is gone by morning, and nobody says where. It is never shown.
     - **Kill him.** Murder in the city is a crime, though no soldier here will report it.
   - **Asclepiodotus** is arrested by Pudens's couriers either way. Casting Caesar's stars is treason, and he is more use alive.
   - **Malchus** is walked to the camp with the Chaldaean by the Father's men.

7. **Pudens** (the camp, by day).
   - **Objective:** **Report to Pudens.**
   - He listens to the end. For once he doesn't reach for a fig. "One of mine. Fifteen years." Then: "The Capena gate, at dawn. Festus told me that himself, in a letter only I read. So Mardonius read my letters too."
   - **On Mardonius:**
     - Brought in: "He'll talk. They all do, eventually. And I'll be the one listening."
     - Left to the Father: "The god's justice. Fine. It's faster than mine."
     - Dead: "Then we'll never know who else he sold."
   - **On "which gate"** (from the cipher): "October. They still need to know the route Caesar will take out of the city. That's what Mardonius was for. Someone else will try to find it now."
   - **Rewards:** 150 denarii; skill XP in Rhetoric, and Locks & Seals or Mercatura for the ways the player took; the board marks **The Eastern Gold: broken** and **In the camp: the leak** solved.

8. **Done.** Banner: **The Eastern Gold is broken**: "Two of four. Pudens wants three."

**History notes.**
- **Mithras in Trajan's Rome.** The cult was young in Rome but present among soldiers and the imperial household; a Trajanic dedication to Mithras by Alcimus, a slave of the praetorian prefect Ti. Claudius Livianus, survives (CIL VI 718). Statius already describes the god "under the rocks of the Persian cave" (*Thebaid* 1.719–20, c. AD 92). The grades of initiation (Raven, Lion, Father…) are attested later; the chapter uses only "Lion" and "Father", lightly.
- **Palmyrenes in Rome.** Palmyrene merchants dedicated a shrine in Trastevere by AD 102. Pepper was stored in Domitian's Horrea Piperataria on the Sacred Way.
- **Astrologers and treason.** Casting the emperor's horoscope was *maiestas*; astrologers were expelled from Rome again and again.
